Genie Hardware — Spec Strategy

Working draft · v0.1 · for discussion

How we decide what physical Genie to build, before we spend on it. Cost-constrained, care-sector, and deliberately comms now, monitoring later. This is a living decision register — same idea as the OPEN Q / DECIDED markers in the broadcast prototype — not a finished spec. Statuses move as answers land.

1 The approach

It isn't business model or feature — pick either alone and the opportunity space stays huge. The two numbers that actually collapse it are the one job the device must do, and who pays and how much. Spec the job, price the buyer, and the spec lives where they meet.

The one job
Get a message to a resident — and sometimes get a confirmation back. (What the prototype already does.) Everything else is "not yet".
→
The cost envelope
Capital £/device we can amortise, and £/resident/month we can charge — all-in. In UK social care this is the master constraint.
→
The spec
The job sets the floor (capability we can't drop below); the envelope sets the ceiling (spend we can't cross). No overlap → the buyer or the job is wrong.

Finding "no overlap" on paper is nearly free. Finding it after tooling is the expensive way. So we resolve as much as possible on the cheap rungs below before committing a penny to custom hardware.

Two cost truths for care hardware

  • The parts bill (BOM) is only half. The half that bites is the recurring & hidden total cost of ownership: connectivity, provisioning, support call-outs, replacements, and certification. A "cheap" device on a costly data plan with frequent support visits is not cheap.
  • "Comms now, monitoring later" is a hardware decision, not a roadmap note. You can't bolt sensors onto a shipped device without re-spinning it. So the rule is: fit nothing for monitoring now, but reserve the headroom and keep a clean regulatory firebreak so monitoring can be added later as a deliberate, separately-certified step (see HW-4 and HW-5).

2 How we test specs quickly — decouple the two hard things

The first-timer trap is thinking "test a spec" means "build a prototype". It doesn't. We're really testing two separate things, and they split cleanly onto cheap surfaces:

  • Does it work for the user? → test in software on borrowed hardware. We already have the mockup.
  • Does it pencil? → test in a spreadsheet (BOM + 3-year TCO), not in silicon.

Only when both point the same way do we climb to real hardware. The ladder — each rung a gate before spending on the next:

  1. Mockup on surrogate devices
    Load the existing prototype on 3–4 off-the-shelf devices (a ~£50 Fire tablet, an Echo Show, a big-button device, a Raspberry Pi + touchscreen). Put them in front of real residents and staff; "Wizard-of-Oz" the unbuilt bits (a human sends the broadcasts).
    Cost: a couple of hundred £, zero engineering. De-risks: modality, screen size, legibility, reach, acknowledge-vs-dismiss.
  2. BOM + TCO spreadsheet
    Model parts cost per candidate spec, plus 3-year recurring (connectivity, support, replacement, certification amortised) across the real deployment size. Each spec becomes a number to rank against the envelope.
    Cost: desk time. De-risks: does any spec actually fit the money.
  3. Dev kit on the chosen compute class
    Run the real software on the target chip/SoM. Confirm performance, connectivity, power, and the reserved sensor headroom (HW-5) hold up.
    Cost: hundreds of £ + engineering. De-risks: the platform choice.
  4. Custom hardware
    Custom PCB spins + enclosure tooling. The point of no cheap return.
    Cost: ~£10k–£100k+ and months. Only past the gate in §4.
Where we are: rung 1 is already possible today — the broadcast prototype is the test rig. Nothing below rung 3 needs a hardware engineer.

3 The decision register — the questions, and how we answer each

Ordered by priority. Each is a question with what's at stake, the realistic options, and — the point of this doc — how we'll answer it and on which rung. "Monitoring-later" notes flag where a choice must protect the future path.

HW-1Build vs buy the compute platform P0Open

The biggest single lever on cost, speed, control, lifecycle — and on how much monitoring headroom we can reserve. It decides almost everything downstream.

Options
a · Off-the-shelf consumer tablet + MDM lockdownb · Commercial care-display / rugged Android SoM in our own enclosurec · Fully custom PCB
How we'll answer it
  1. Rung 1 surrogate trial to prove the interaction is platform-agnostic (it should run anywhere).
  2. Rung 2 BOM/TCO comparing (a)/(b)/(c) over 3 years at target fleet size.
  3. A build-vs-buy scorecard: unit cost, lead time, control, security-update lifespan, and headroom for HW-5.
Monitoring-later: a locked consumer tablet (a) usually can't host a sensor bus → a later re-spin. An SoM (b) keeps GPIO/expansion and is the safe default for preserving the path.
HW-2Interaction modality — screen, voice, or ambient P0Leaning: screen

The acknowledgement flow needs a visible surface and a deliberate confirm. Voice-only confirmation is unreliable for elderly users in noisy rooms; an ambient light-and-button can't carry a 200-character message body. The prototype already assumes a screen.

How we'll answer it

Rung 1: load the mockup on a Fire tablet, an Echo Show (screen + voice), and a big-button device; sit with 5–8 residents and staff. Measure: can they find it, read it (font size, contrast, viewing distance), and complete acknowledge vs dismiss unaided? Output: confirm screen, and the minimum size/legibility spec.

HW-3Connectivity — WiFi, cellular, or hybrid P0Open

The TCO killer, and the one most likely to blow the envelope. Care and elderly homes frequently lack reliable WiFi in residents' rooms and have nobody to administer it. Cellular "just works" but adds a modem plus a recurring data/SIM cost forever, and its own management (APNs, multi-network SIMs).

Options
WiFi-onlyCellular-only (managed multi-network SIM)Hybrid — WiFi with cellular failover
How we'll answer it
  1. Survey 5–10 target settings by phone/desk: is there WiFi, is it reliable in the room, who administers it?
  2. Rung 2 TCO: cellular £/device/mo × fleet × 36 months vs the support-call cost of WiFi drop-outs. (Indicative cellular ≈ £3–8/device/mo — validate; model against the £/resident/mo the business model allows.)
  3. Two target-operator interviews to sanity-check reality over spec sheets.
Monitoring-later: monitoring raises data volume and uptime needs — size the data path now; a hybrid path de-risks both the comms job today and monitoring later.
HW-4Regulatory classification & the monitoring firebreak P0Open

The linchpin of "comms now, monitoring later." Comms/reminders sit on a light path (UKCA/CE electrical + EMC, UK GDPR/DSPT, and DTAC if selling to the NHS). Fall detection or health monitoring can make the device a medical device (UK MDR, MHRA) — a different universe of cost and time. We want to stay firmly on the comms side now while keeping a clean, deliberate route to cross later.

How we'll answer it
  1. Desk-map the current job against MHRA "is my product a medical device?" and borderline/software guidance.
  2. Commission a light regulatory opinion (cheap next to a re-spin) confirming comms-only is out of scope, and defining the firebreak.
  3. Design the firebreak architecturally: monitoring as a separately certifiable add-on module/service, so the base device never inherits medical-device obligations.
  4. Capture DTAC + DSPT requirements now — they apply regardless of monitoring.
Monitoring-later: this question is the future path. Get the firebreak wrong and either we over-regulate the comms device today, or we can't add monitoring later without recertifying the whole product.
HW-5Sensor & compute headroom to reserve now P0Open

"Monitoring later" is only cheap if the day-one hardware already reserves what monitoring will need — otherwise it's a re-spin. But over-reserving wastes budget we don't have. We need the minimum reservation, no more.

How we'll answer it
  1. Desk study: shortlist the realistic later features (room presence/occupancy, fall detection, environmental temp/humidity, medication adherence) and their hardware demands.
  2. Turn that into a reserve list: spare compute/RAM, a sensor bus/expansion header, power budget, physical placement suited to sensing, and a data path sized for it.
  3. Feed the reserve list into the HW-1 scorecard; validate on a Rung 3 dev kit under load.

Principle: reserve interfaces and headroom, don't fit sensors.

HW-6Power — mains or battery P1Leaning: mains-first

Battery + frail users is a known failure mode: an uncharged device is an offline device is a missed message — a safeguarding gap. Mains is always-on and simplest, but constrains where the device can live.

How we'll answer it

Rung 1 field observation of where the device naturally sits and socket availability. Expected outcome: mains-first, with a small ride-through (so a plug knocked out degrades gracefully rather than hard-dropping) — not battery for portability.

HW-7Provisioning, fleet management & lifecycle P1Open

At fleet scale the recurring cost is enrolment, remote update/lockdown, device-health monitoring, RMA/replacement, and how long security updates are supported. A major TCO lever and a safeguarding matter — a dead device is a missed message.

How we'll answer it
  1. Choose an MDM/fleet approach consistent with the HW-1 outcome.
  2. Model truck-roll / support cost in the Rung 2 TCO.
  3. Require zero-touch enrolment, remote recovery, and a minimum supported-life / security-update horizon in the platform scorecard.
HW-8Data, security & privacy architecture P1Open

DSPT and UK GDPR apply from day one. A comms device stores little; monitoring would add sensitive data. Designing the split now keeps the comms product light and ties directly to the HW-4 firebreak.

How we'll answer it

Desk-design the data flows (at-rest and in-transit); complete a DSPT self-assessment mapping; design any future monitoring data as a distinct, consent-gated, separately-governed stream rather than folded into the comms data model.

HW-9Enclosure, industrial design & mounting P2Open

Elderly-first ergonomics: big targets, high contrast, glare/viewing angle, tremor tolerance, wipe-clean (IP rating), robust, and hard to unplug by accident or walk off with. Table vs wall mount.

How we'll answer it

Rung 1 form-factor trials + care-staff interviews on placement and robustness. Defer tooling to Rung 4. Keep the enclosure decoupled from the compute choice so industrial design can iterate without re-spinning electronics.

HW-10Audio — speaker and microphone P2Leaning: speaker + basic mic

Needed for the device to "light up and speak," plus accessibility for hard-of-hearing users. A far-field mic array adds cost, and — with monitoring — raises acoustic-sensing and privacy questions.

How we'll answer it

Rung 1 audibility test in a real room. Decide mic spec against whether voice input is in the day-one job — currently it isn't (confirmation is a tap), so far-field only if voice interaction proves needed.

4 What to do next — cheap, parallel, ~2–4 weeks

  1. Write the one job (one sentence) and the two envelope numbers — capital £/device you can amortise, and £/resident/month you can charge all-in. This is the commercial half; nothing specs cleanly without it.
  2. Buy 3–4 surrogate devices (~£300) and run the existing mockup with 5–8 residents and staff — settles HW-2, and informs HW-6, HW-7, HW-9, HW-10.
  3. Stand up the BOM/TCO spreadsheet, even rough — turns HW-1, HW-3 and HW-7 from opinions into numbers.
  4. Commission a light regulatory opinion and start DTAC/DSPT mapping — HW-4 and HW-8, the "monitoring-later" guardrails.
  5. Desk study: monitoring-feature shortlist → reserve list — HW-5, so HW-1 protects the future path.
The gate to custom tooling. Only spend on custom PCB/enclosure tooling once all of these hold: interaction validated on surrogates · a spec's 3-year TCO fits the envelope with margin · regulatory firebreak confirmed · reserve-list validated on a dev kit · a named launch customer or deployment. Until then, stay on the cheap rungs.

Numbers here are indicative and flagged to validate — same honesty as the prototype's "working defaults, to confirm in UAT."
